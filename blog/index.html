Ciencia del mar, humor y autogestión

November 14th 2026

La ecología social de Murray Bookchin me sirve para preguntar quién decide cómo habitamos la costa, quién puede producir conocimiento sobre el océano y cómo lo ponemos en común.

En Anarcomedia, junto con Milo Allende, llevamos esas preguntas a espacios autogestionados de stand-up. Los bares, los micros abiertos y los espacios barriales permiten probar una idea frente a gente que puede discutirla desde su propia experiencia. El remate funciona cuando deja ver una contradicción y abre la conversación.

En el seminario «Enraizar el sur Austral» presenté «No se trata de salvar el océano». La pregunta que llevé a ese encuentro sobre salud mental, ciencia y territorio fue cómo aprender a cuidarnos mientras aprendemos a observar el mar. Eso también implica conversar sobre quién toma las decisiones y qué saberes pueden entrar en ellas.

Audience research with AI and nearest neighbours

December 4th 2023

I used an AI assistant to draft questions and organise hypotheses about a potential audience. A profile is useful when it can be checked against interviews, behavioural evidence or other data you have permission to use.

With labelled examples, nearest neighbours can help assign new records to an existing group. The result depends on what gets measured, how features are scaled and how distance is defined. Test with examples held out of training and compare with a simple baseline before using predictions to make decisions.

I’d use those results to decide what to investigate next and which questions an outreach campaign still needs to answer.

Exploring a dataset with Data Wrangler

December 2nd 2023

In this exercise I used Data Wrangler in VS Code to inspect column types, missing values and distributions before cleaning a dataset. The visual interface let me try replacements and group summaries and see what changed.

The part I’d carry into another project is the Python code produced for each operation. I can review it, add checks and keep it with the analysis so the transformations can be rerun. Decisions about missing values still need to be documented.

Choosing a model for the question

November 25th 2023

I start with a baseline that I can explain. Regression can be a useful first comparison. Nearest neighbours depend on how distance is defined. Trees can model interactions, and random forests or boosting are alternatives when a single tree misses useful structure.

The task matters. K-means identifies groups without labels. ARIMA and exponential smoothing address forecasting from a time series. Each needs its own checks. For observations that are close in time or space, I set aside entire periods or regions when that matches how the model will be used.

My general portfolio includes examples of segmentation and classification.

The work of working together

November 25th 2023

In research, teamwork shows up in small things. Can someone else reproduce my figure? Do we know who checked a result? Have we left time to hear a disagreement before agreeing on an interpretation?

I try to write down what I know, what I’m unsure about and what someone else will need to pick up the work. Listening, managing time and owning mistakes make those habits possible.

Anarcomedia, science and bars

November 15th 2023

When public funds pay for research, I think researchers have a responsibility to share how they work and what they learn. In Chile I found that a stand-up show could start that conversation with people who rarely walk into a university lecture.

A comic has to write, try material in an open mic, hear what the audience catches and rewrite. Finding a venue, putting together a bill and getting people into the room are part of the work. Anarcomedia grew out of that practice in the Chilean stand-up circuit.

My later note on ocean science, humour and self-management shows how I’ve carried the idea into more recent work.

What stand-up taught me about science communication

November 15th 2023

Stand-up gave me a practical way to find out whether an explanation reaches the people in the room. A joke can hold enough of an idea for an audience to recognise it and then expose where that idea falls short.

In bars and public spaces the people listening bring their own experiences and questions. That changes what I say and what I understand about a subject. I think of that exchange as part of doing science in public.

I carry that idea into Anarcomedia, where humour helps open up conversations about the sea.

Ciencia y humor

November 15th 2023

In 2019, Diego Torres and I gave a talk in the Geophysics Lecture Series called Ciencia y humor. We compared how jokes and scientific arguments are built and looked at examples of humour in science communication.
